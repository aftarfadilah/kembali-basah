# Kembali Basah — Brand Profile

> **Concept project.** Kembali Basah is a fictional peatland restoration foundation, created as a frontend case study by [Aftar Fadilah](https://aftarfadilah.com). It does not exist as an organisation, takes no donations, and is not affiliated with any real foundation, agency or company. Every page that shows programme results, finances or donation flows must say so (see *Trust rules*).

## 1. The idea

Peat burns when it is dry and mostly does not when it is wet. Much of Indonesia's peat was drained by canals, so it became fuel. **Rewetting** reverses that: block the canals, raise the water table, and the peat is saturated again.

*Kembali basah* means "wet again". The name is the intervention.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Kembali Basah (kem-BAH-lee BAH-sah) |
| **Legal form (fictional)** | Yayasan Kembali Basah |
| **Tagline, Indonesian** | Gambut basah, api padam. |
| **Tagline, English** | Keep the peat wet. Keep the fire out. |
| **Category** | Peatland restoration and fire prevention |
| **Default language** | Indonesian, with full English |

## 2. Mission, vision, belief

- **Mission:** help peat communities keep their peatland wet, and make the fire risk around them visible.
- **Vision:** peat landscapes where water stays in the ground and fire season stops being an annual emergency.
- **Belief:** prevention is cheaper and kinder than firefighting, and the people who live on peat are the most capable people to protect it.

## 3. What the foundation does (the programmes)

These are concept programmes. Names are fixed so the site, copy and UI use them consistently.

| Programme | Indonesian | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Canal blocking** | Sekat Kanal | Building dams in drainage canals so water stays in the peat. |
| **Fire watch** | Pantau Api | Public satellite monitoring of hotspots, built on open NASA FIRMS data. |
| **Village fire teams** | Regu Siaga Api | Training and equipping village teams to prevent and respond early. |
| **Wet-peat farming** | Tani Basah | Supporting crops that grow in wet peat, so people don't need to drain it. |

## 4. Who we speak to

1. **Peat villagers and local partners.** Practical, respectful, mobile-first, often on slow connections. Need: clear programme information and a way to reach us.
2. **Supporters.** Individuals who want to help and need to trust where the help goes. Need: honest numbers, a clear action, no guilt.
3. **Researchers, journalists, partners.** Need: sources, methods, downloadable data.

## 5. Personality

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| **Grounded.** Specific, practical, rooted in place. | Preachy, apocalyptic, "save the planet" |
| **Calm.** We lower the temperature. Water settles things. | Panicked, sensational, disaster imagery |
| **Honest.** We show our sources and our limits. | Inflated, vague about impact |
| **Local.** Indonesian first, written naturally. | Translated from English, generic NGO |

## 6. Voice and tone

Short sentences. One idea each. Real numbers with a source, or no number. Say what the reader can do in one line.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Together we can save our precious planet!" | "Block one canal. Keep one village's peat wet." |
| "Devastating fires are destroying everything." | "Dry peat burns for weeks, even underground." |
| "Join the movement today!" | "Support a canal block." |

- **Tone shifts:** warmer on stories and thank-yous, plainer on data and finances, calmest on anything about fire.
- **Indonesian copy is written first**, then adapted into English. It can run around 15% longer, so layouts must allow for it.
- **Never use fear or guilt as the lever.** Never imply a donation outcome we can't show.

## 7. Messaging pillars

1. **Wet peat doesn't burn.** The cause, in one line.
2. **Prevention beats firefighting.** Why we work upstream.
3. **We show our work.** Open data, public numbers, named sources.
4. **People protect what they live on.** Villages lead.

## 8. Trust rules (these are hard rules for every page and component)

1. Every page states it is a concept project, in a persistent footer note and wherever results or money appear.
2. No invented statistics. Illustrative figures are labelled **"Contoh / Example"** and visually marked.
3. Fire data always names its source and date: NASA FIRMS, VIIRS S-NPP, with the snapshot or refresh time.
4. The donation flow is a UI demonstration. It never takes payment details.
5. Photography of real people and places is not used unless it is licensed and credited. The default visual language is illustration and data.

## 9. Visual identity at a glance

Full detail is in [design-guidelines.md](design-guidelines.md).

- **Mark:** a drop containing a wavy water-table line, with a single ember above it. Water meets fire.
- **Colour:** peat browns and warm paper as the base, tannin (blackwater amber) for water, moss for recovery, and ember reserved strictly for fire data.
- **Type:** Fraunces for headlines, Public Sans for interface and body, IBM Plex Mono for data.
- **Signature interaction:** the *Rewet* slider turns real satellite points from ember to moss. It is a concept illustration, not a measured result.

## 10. Brand rules in one list

1. Ember means fire, and nothing else.
2. Moss means rewetted or recovering, and nothing else.
3. Never show a fire without the way out beside it (the action or the programme).
4. Water settles, fire sharpens: calm easing for water, snappy for fire.
5. Indonesian first.
6. Data is the imagery.
